# Technical Disclosure Addendum — Hierarchical Exterior Air Renewal

Date: 2026-08-19  
Related numerical experiment: `docs/e3-boundary-layer-screen.md`

## Purpose

This addendum makes explicit an exterior architecture identified during boundary-layer screening: a high-area wet microstructure may be combined with a second, larger geometric scale whose purpose is to renew ambient air near the evaporation surfaces.

The architecture is applicable to the primary fanless garment and does not require onboard powered airflow.

## Representative structure

A garment may comprise, from skin side toward ambient:

1. a directional liquid-transport textile;
2. a flexible continuous or patterned in-plane heat-spreader;
3. one or more capillary liquid-distribution paths;
4. wet microstructured evaporation fields including ribs, short fins, 3-D knit relief, pile, loops, lamellae, scales, pleats, or combinations;
5. macro-scale air-renewal paths positioned adjacent to, between, above, or through the wet microstructured fields.

The macro-scale paths may include:

- open longitudinal or transverse corridors;
- valleys between discontinuous evaporator islands;
- spacer-knit channels;
- grooves or corrugations;
- pleat gaps;
- perforated stand-off layers;
- branching channels;
- raised rails that maintain an air gap under compression;
- combinations of these structures.

## Scale separation

The microstructure and macrostructure need not have the same pitch or height.

Representative research ranges, without limiting the disclosure, include:

- micro-rib/fin height: approximately 0.2–10 mm;
- micro-rib/fin pitch: approximately 0.2–10 mm;
- macro corridor width: approximately 0.5–20 mm;
- evaporator-field width between macro corridors: approximately 2–100 mm;
- structured exterior coverage: approximately 10–100% of active projected garment area.

A current bench-test subset uses microstructure height around 2–3 mm, local pitch around 0.8–1.5 mm, macro corridor width around 1–6 mm, and evaporator-field width around 5–25 mm.

These are test ranges, not optimized product dimensions.

## Wetting variants

Macro air-renewal paths may be:

- intentionally dry;
- partially wet;
- fully wet;
- intermittently wet;
- supplied by separate capillary branches;
- covered with lower-conductivity material where dry hot-air heat pickup is a concern.

The wet microstructure may receive liquid continuously or intermittently.

## Air-renewal mechanisms

Air renewal may arise from one or more of:

- buoyancy/natural convection;
- wearer motion;
- garment pumping during bending or walking;
- ambient wind;
- vehicle or cycling airflow;
- external stationary fans;
- optional onboard fans in a secondary active embodiment.

The primary embodiment remains operable without an onboard fan.

## Orientation variants

Macro paths may be aligned:

- vertically with gravity;
- horizontally;
- diagonally;
- circumferentially around a limb or torso;
- radially from a central evaporator region;
- along seams or visual garment patterns;
- parallel or perpendicular to the high-conductivity axis of an anisotropic heat spreader.

Different orientations may be used on different garment zones.

## Thermal integration

The heat spreader may thermally feed the wet microstructured fields while dry macro-corridor regions are thermally shielded from hot ambient air. The architecture may use high in-plane conductivity together with lower through-thickness conductivity, segmented conductive paths, insulating covers over dry regions, or other measures that reduce parasitic inward heat gain.

## Design objective

The design objective is not merely to increase geometric area. It is to increase the amount of **wetted evaporation area that remains coupled to refreshed ambient air and to body-derived heat**.

The E3 pure-diffusion screen suggests that this distinction can dominate the benefit of sub-millimeter pitch refinement when a dense wet field is buried inside one shared humid boundary layer. Physical testing is required to determine how real natural convection, motion and 3-D textile geometry modify that result.
