# Pressure-aware terminal placement with heat/liquid route regularization

Status: **SIMULATION / VIRTUAL PROTOTYPE ONLY — NO PHYSICAL SPECIMEN**

## Question

Pure pressure avoidance moves active wet area away from loaded regions, but this can lengthen both liquid routes from sweat collection points and heat routes from loaded/dry regions.

This screen therefore treats terminal placement as a multi-objective problem rather than maximizing the pressure-map thermal output alone.

## Metrics

For each equal-area wet mask under the synthetic backpack+strap map:

1. body-side heat flux from the spatial pressure thermal model — maximize;
2. 95th-percentile nearest-terminal liquid distance — minimize;
3. load-weighted RMS nearest-terminal heat-routing distance — minimize.

No arbitrary scalar weighting is used to declare one universal optimum; Pareto candidates are retained.

The route-distance metrics are geometric proxies. They are not full hydraulic or thermal-network solutions.

## Current tradeoff

The pure pressure-focused placement gives the highest thermal result but the longest routes among the strongest candidates.

Representative converged 24 x 24 values:

| candidate | body-side heat flux | liquid p95 distance | load-weighted heat RMS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| pressure-focused | ~112.8 W/m² | ~20.2 mm | ~13.0 mm |
| regularized (`beta=0.125`, route weight 0.35) | ~110.8 | ~16.8 mm | ~10.4 mm |
| peripheral islands | ~106.4 | ~15.8 mm | ~9.9 mm |
| four islands | ~84.3 | ~10.6 mm | ~6.4 mm |

The regularized candidate gives up roughly 1–2% of thermal output relative to the pressure-focused candidate while reducing the route-distance proxies by about 17–20%.

## Placement rule

The explored score is

\[
S(x,y)=[1-p(x,y)] + \beta T_4(x,y) + w_r R(x,y),
\]

where

- `p` is normalized load pressure;
- `T4` is a distributed four-island template;
- `R` is the routed high-`k` network mask;
- `beta` regularizes against over-clustering purely in the lowest-pressure region;
- `w_r` rewards terminal overlap with the heat-routing network.

This is a heuristic search family, not a topology optimum.

## Design implication

The baseline should no longer be described simply as "put evaporators at the lowest-pressure cells." A better current rule is:

> avoid persistent pressure while maintaining a distributed set of terminals close enough to local liquid sources and loaded/dry heat sources that route burdens remain short.

Thus the garment should use multiple regional terminal groups, not one low-pressure evaporator cluster.

## Limits

- nearest-terminal distance ignores manifold topology and obstacles;
- vertical liquid lift is not included in this 2-D distance metric;
- heat distance is a proxy even though conductive burden scales with geometry/material/contact rather than distance alone;
- wet source distribution is taken as spatially uniform in the distance metric;
- the pressure map is static and synthetic;
- candidate masks are generated by a simple score threshold rather than combinatorial/topology optimization.

## Next work

1. replace geometric liquid distance with capillary pressure/area cost from the liquid-network model;
2. replace heat distance with explicit `k*t/contact` route cost;
3. allow local sweat-source intensity and load maps to vary regionally;
4. optimize multiple fixed terminal groups on a garment-scale domain;
5. include curvature and seams.
